Part 4: The Scientific Revolution¶

Part 4 covers the last 500 years (from c. 1500 CE to the present): how admitting "we don't know" ignited a feedback loop of science, empire, and capitalism, transformed energy and daily life, and now brings Homo sapiens to the threshold of engineering its own successors.

Chapter 14: The Discovery of Ignorance¶

The Big Idea: The Scientific Revolution was not a revolution of knowledge; it was a revolution of ignorance. Progress exploded once humans admitted they did not know the answers to the most important questions and started using observation and maths to find out.

Core Concepts & Notes¶

  • The Explosive Scale of the Last 500 Years:
    • In 1500: ~500 million humans, global production ~$250 billion, daily energy consumption ~13 trillion calories.
    • Today: ~8 billion humans, global production >$100 trillion, daily energy consumption >1,500 trillion calories.
  • Pre-Modern Knowledge vs. Modern Science: Pre-modern traditions (the Bible, the Quran, the Vedas, Confucian classics) assumed everything worth knowing was already revealed in ancient scriptures. Modern science rests on three radical pillars:
    1. Willingness to admit ignorance (ignoramus, meaning "we do not know").
    2. Centrality of observation and mathematics (replacing stories with statistical and mathematical models).
    3. Acquisition of new powers (knowledge is not just truth; it is technology that cures diseases, builds weapons, and powers economies).
  • The Gilgamesh Project (Conquering Death): Before science, death was viewed as an inevitable metaphysical decree. Modern science treats death as a technical problem (a heart stops pumping, a cell mutates, an artery clogs) that can eventually be delayed or solved.
  • Science Is Never Free: Research is expensive. Scientists rarely decide what gets studied on pure curiosity alone; governments, empires, and capitalists fund science in exchange for economic growth, military superiority, and ideological goals.

Mind-Blowing Anecdotes & Stories¶

  • The Spider That Broke Ancient Scripture: If a medieval peasant asked how spiders weave webs, a priest would say: if the Bible doesn't mention it, it's not worth knowing. Modern science flipped that on its head: admitting you don't know how a spider makes silk is the starting gun for materials science.
  • The Two Scottish Ministers Who Invented Actuarial Finance (1744): Alexander Webster and Robert Wallace wanted to set up a life insurance fund for widows of Scottish clergymen. Instead of praying for God's blessing, they gathered mortality data and used Edmond Halley's statistical probability tables to calculate exact premiums. They predicted that by 1765 the fund's capital would be £58,348; in 1765 the actual capital was £58,347 (off by a single pound!). That fund still exists today as Scottish Widows.
  • The First Atomic Bomb (Alamogordo, 16 July 1945): The ultimate proof that science is about power, not just abstract truth. For the first time in history, humanity gained the capability to end history itself.

Memorable Quotes¶

"The Scientific Revolution has not been a revolution of knowledge. It has been above all a revolution of ignorance. The great discovery that launched the Scientific Revolution was the discovery that humans do not know the answers to their most important questions."

"For men of science, death is not an inevitable destiny, but merely a technical problem."

"Science is unable to set its own priorities. It is also incapable of determining what to do with its discoveries."

Chapter 15: The Marriage of Science and Empire¶

The Big Idea: Why did tiny, backward Europe conquer the world instead of far richer China, India, or the Ottoman Empire? Because Europe paired scientific curiosity with imperial ambition, sending scientists alongside soldiers on every voyage.

Core Concepts & Notes¶

  • The Power Shift (1500 vs. 1850): In 1500, Europe was a backwater; Asia produced 80% of the world's economic output. By 1850, Britain alone ruled India and humiliated China in the Opium Wars. Europe's advantage wasn't superior weapons in 1500; it was a scientific and capitalist mindset that compounded over three centuries.
  • Empty Maps (The Psychological Breakthrough): Pre-modern maps filled unknown oceans with sea monsters and neat borders, pretending the world was already known. Starting around 1459–1500, European mapmakers began drawing blank spaces on maps: a visual admission of ignorance and an open invitation to go explore and conquer.
  • Scientists on Warships: European empires did not just conquer; they catalogued every plant, language, ruin, and coastline. Conquest funded science, and science (better navigation, quinine for malaria, accurate maps, steamships, linguistics) made conquest vastly easier.
  • The Feedback Loop: Political/economic institutions fund science -> science yields new tech and resources -> resources expand the empire -> empire funds even more science.

Mind-Blowing Anecdotes & Stories¶

  • Amerigo Vespucci vs. Christopher Columbus: Columbus died believing he had reached small islands off East Asia because he couldn't imagine an entire unknown continent outside scripture. Amerigo Vespucci was the first to admit "we don't know what this is; it's a New World", which is why mapmaker Martin Waldseemüller named the landmass America after him, not Columbus.
  • Captain James Cook's Voyage (1768): Science or Conquest? Cook sailed to Tahiti ostensibly to observe the transit of Venus (to calculate the distance from Earth to the Sun), carrying astronomers, botanists, and artists. On the exact same voyage, he mapped New Zealand and Australia, claimed Australia for Britain, and pioneered the use of sauerkraut and citrus to beat scurvy (saving thousands of sailors and enabling Britain to rule the oceans). Was the Endeavour a scientific expedition or a military one? Both; they were inseparable.
  • Admiral Zheng He vs. Europe: Decades before Columbus, China's Ming Dynasty sent Admiral Zheng He across the Indian Ocean with 300 massive ships and 30,000 sailors (dwarfing Columbus's 3 tiny boats). Yet China had no interest in conquering or mapping unknown worlds; when politics shifted in Beijing, the fleet was dismantled and forgotten. Europe lacked China's wealth, so it had the hunger, the empty maps, and the mindset to conquer.
  • Sir William Jones & Henry Rawlinson (Deciphering India and Babylon): British officials in Asia didn't just collect taxes; Sir William Jones discovered the link between Sanskrit, Latin, and Greek (founding modern linguistics), and Henry Rawlinson risked his life dangling off the Behistun Cliff in Persia to copy cuneiform inscriptions, unlocking 3,000 years of lost Mesopotamian history.

Memorable Quotes¶

"The discovery of America was the foundational event of the Scientific Revolution. It not only taught Europeans to favour present observations over past traditions, but the desire to conquer America also obliged Europeans to search for new knowledge at breakneck speed."

"Both the scientist and the conqueror began by admitting ignorance; they both said 'I don't know what's out there.'"

Chapter 16: The Capitalist Creed¶

The Big Idea: Modern economic growth rests on credit (trust in the future). Once humans believed the economic pie could grow forever, capitalism turned reinvestment of profits into a moral creed, fuelling both staggering prosperity and brutal imperial exploitation.

Core Concepts & Notes¶

  • The Magic of Credit (Trust in Tomorrow): For most of history, the economic pie was seen as fixed (if I get richer, you must get poorer). Because nobody expected the future to be richer than the present, credit was scarce, interest rates were sky-high, and growth stalled. The Scientific Revolution sparked belief in progress (the idea that tomorrow's pie will be bigger), unlocking cheap credit, which funded new businesses, which actually made tomorrow's pie bigger.
  • Adam Smith's Revolutionary Idea (1776): Greed is good if profits are reinvested. When a shoemaker reinvests profits to hire more workers and build a bigger factory, his self-interest grows the whole economy.
  • Capital vs. Wealth: A medieval king hoarding gold in a treasure chest is wealthy, not a capitalist. A capitalist puts money to work in production to generate more capital.
  • The Capitalist-Imperial Marriage: Joint-stock companies (like the Dutch VOC and British East India Company) allowed ordinary investors to pool risk, fund private armies, conquer entire countries, and build global trade empires.
  • The Dark Side of Unchecked Free Markets: Left completely unregulated by states and ethics, capitalism treats human lives as disposable inputs (the Atlantic slave trade was funded by European stock markets and banks, not by kings or priests).

Mind-Blowing Anecdotes & Stories¶

  • The Bakery Loan Thought Experiment: A baker wants to open a bakery, and a contractor wants to build it, using $1 million borrowed from a bank that holds a $1 million deposit. On paper, $2 million now exists in the economy even though only $1 million of cash sits in the vault. The entire modern economy runs on this trust in a future bakery that doesn't exist yet.
  • The Mississippi Bubble (1717–1720): Scottish financier John Law persuaded France to invest in the Mississippi Company, promising untold riches in the Louisiana swamps. Speculators drove shares to absurd heights until reality hit, the bubble burst, the French economy collapsed, and the resulting debt crisis helped pave the way to the French Revolution and let Britain overtake France as the world's superpower.
  • A Private Corporation Conquered India & Indonesia: Indonesia was conquered not by the Dutch government, but by a private joint-stock company (the Dutch East India Company / VOC), which hired mercenaries, built forts, and ruled for nearly 200 years. India was likewise conquered by the British East India Company, which at its peak fielded a private army of 350,000 soldiers, larger than the British Royal Army!
  • The Opium Wars (1839–1842): When China tried to ban British merchants from flooding the country with addictive opium, the British Navy went to war to defend "free trade", forced China to legalise the drug, and took Hong Kong. Millions of Chinese were addicted so British shareholders could make a profit.

Memorable Quotes¶

"Credit enables us to build the present at the expense of the future. It's founded on the assumption that our future resources are sure to be far more abundant than our present resources."

"The Atlantic slave trade did not stem from racist hatred towards Africans. The individuals who bought the shares, the brokers who sold them, and the managers of the slave-trade companies rarely thought about the Africans."

Chapter 17: The Wheels of Industry¶

The Big Idea: The Industrial Revolution was at its core a Second Agricultural Revolution and an energy-conversion revolution: we learned to turn heat into motion, broke free from muscle power, and turned humans into clock-watching consumers.

Core Concepts & Notes¶

  • The Energy Conversion Breakthrough: For millennia, almost all energy came from human and animal muscles (ultimately sunlight -> plants -> muscles). The steam engine (and later the internal combustion engine and electricity) cracked the secret of converting heat into kinetic movement, unlocking vast oceans of fossil-fuel energy.
  • We Are Not Running Out of Energy, Only Knowledge: The Sun pours more energy onto Earth every 90 minutes than humanity uses in a year. Every historical energy crisis was solved by discovering how to tap a new source (coal, oil, nuclear, solar).
  • Industrialised Agriculture & Factory Farming: Tractors, synthetic fertilisers, and antibiotics meant fewer than 2% of the US population could feed the entire country. The tragic flip side: billions of sentient farm animals (pigs, cows, chickens) were reduced to biological machines on factory assembly lines, arguably the worst crime in history by sheer volume of suffering.
  • The Age of Shopping (Consumerism): Industrial factories produce far more than people actually need. To prevent economic collapse, a new ethic had to replace millennia of frugality: Consumerism, the only religion whose followers actually do what they are told ("Treat yourself! Buy more!").
  • Obesity as a Double Victory for Consumerism: Eat too much junk food -> buy diet products, gym memberships, and weight-loss drugs. The economy wins twice!

Mind-Blowing Anecdotes & Stories¶

  • The Invention of Uniform Time (1847–1880): Before railways, every town set its own local noon by the sun (Bristol was 10 minutes behind London, and nobody cared). Once trains ran at 60 km/h, mismatched local clocks caused missed connections and crashes. In 1847 British railways forced everyone onto Greenwich Mean Time, and in 1880 Parliament made it national law. Industrial factories and timetables trained humanity to live by the wristwatch instead of the sun and seasons.
  • Harry Harlow's Monkey Experiments (1950s): To show why factory farming is psychologically cruel even when animals are fed and kept disease-free, Harari cites Harlow's infamous experiments: baby rhesus monkeys separated from their mothers were given two fake "mothers": one made of wire holding a milk bottle, the other covered in soft cloth with no milk. The babies clung desperately to the cloth mother for comfort and warmth, proving mammals have deep emotional and social needs far beyond calories.

Memorable Quotes¶

"The Industrial Revolution was, at its heart, a revolution in energy conversion."

"Consumerism is the first religion in history whose followers actually do what they are asked to do."

"Industrial agriculture may well be the greatest crime in history."

Chapter 18: A Permanent Revolution¶

The Big Idea: In just two centuries, the state and the market replaced the ancient family and local community, while humanity achieved an unprecedented era of global peace, even as we trigger a massive ecological crisis.

Core Concepts & Notes¶

  • The Collapse of Family and Community: For 99% of human history, the extended family and local village were your welfare system, police, school, hospital, bank, and marriage broker. Over the last 200 years, the State and the Market took over all those roles, offering individuals freedom ("you can marry whoever you want and move to any city") in exchange for becoming dependent on impersonal institutions and imagined communities (nations and consumer tribes).
  • Permanent Change Is the New Normal: Pre-modern societies assumed the social order was eternal. Today, social structures, jobs, and norms mutate every decade; the only constant is change itself.
  • The Unexpected Era of Peace: Despite headlines, we live in the most peaceful era in human history:
    • Violence dropped from ~15% of adult deaths in ancient foraging societies to <1% today.
    • Today you are far more likely to die from suicide or obesity/sugar than from a soldier, terrorist, or criminal.
    • Why did war stop paying? Four reasons: (1) Nuclear weapons made war between superpowers collective suicide; (2) Wealth shifted from tangible land/wheat/gold mines to human capital and tech (you can't conquer Silicon Valley with tanks; the engineers would flee and the IP would vanish); (3) Trade became far more lucrative than conquest; (4) Global political culture turned against war.
  • Ecological Turmoil (Not Destruction of Nature): Nature itself isn't going to vanish (rats, cockroaches, and bacteria will thrive); what is threatened is the specific ecological stability that Homo sapiens and current wildlife depend on.

Mind-Blowing Anecdotes & Stories¶

  • Why China Doesn't Invade Silicon Valley: In ancient times, if Rome conquered Egypt, it immediately captured Egypt's wealth (fertile wheat fields and slaves). Today, California's wealth is silicon, software, and research networks. If a foreign army marched into Silicon Valley, there are no gold mines to loot; the value evaporates the moment the tanks roll in.
  • Sugar Is Deadlier Than Gunpowder: In 2012, roughly 56 million people died worldwide: 620,000 died from human violence (120,000 in war, 500,000 from crime), while 800,000 committed suicide and 1.5 million died from diabetes. Coca-Cola poses a far bigger statistical threat to your life than Al-Qaeda.

Memorable Quotes¶

"Most people don't appreciate just how peaceful an era we live in."

"Today you are more likely to die from eating too much at McDonald's than from drought, Ebola, or an Al-Qaeda attack."

"The state and the market are the mother and father of the individual, and the individual can survive only thanks to them."

Chapter 19: And They Lived Happily Ever After¶

The Big Idea: Humanity has multiplied its power and wealth a hundredfold, yet we have no real proof that modern humans are any happier than ancient foragers or medieval peasants.

Core Concepts & Notes¶

  • The Blind Spot of History: Historians track GDP, empires, and inventions, but almost never ask the most important question: Did all this progress actually make people happier?
  • What Actually Drives Happiness?
    1. Expectations (The Hedonic Treadmill): Happiness is not objective wealth or health; it is reality minus expectations. As life gets better, our expectations skyrocket (medieval peasants were thrilled to survive a winter; modern commuters get furious when Wi-Fi drops for two minutes).
    2. Biochemistry: Biologists argue our happiness is governed by serotonin, dopamine, and oxytocin: an internal "thermostat" set largely by genetics. Winning the lottery or losing a leg shifts happiness temporarily, before the biochemical thermostat pulls you back to your baseline.
    3. Meaning (The Narrative Self): People will endure immense pain if they believe their lives have a meaningful story (raising children, serving a cause, religious devotion). As Nietzsche said: "He who has a why to live can bear almost any how." Even if the meaning is an imagined myth, it still produces genuine psychological satisfaction.
    4. The Buddhist View ("Know Thyself"): Both chasing pleasant sensations (biochemistry) and clinging to illusions (meaning) keep us trapped on a treadmill. True happiness comes from understanding the impermanent nature of feelings and letting go of craving.

Mind-Blowing Anecdotes & Stories¶

  • The Medieval Peasant vs. The Modern Parisian: A medieval French peasant who didn't own a flushing toilet didn't feel miserable about it because he had never imagined one existed. A modern Parisian who suddenly loses indoor plumbing for a week feels devastated. Better living conditions raise the bar for happiness just as fast as they satisfy old desires.
  • The Lottery Winner & The Paraplegic: Psychological studies repeatedly find that a year after winning the lottery (or suffering a life-altering injury), most people return surprisingly close to their pre-event baseline of happiness. If biochemistry runs the show, the only way to permanently upgrade human happiness may not be political revolution, but manipulating our own biochemistry.

Memorable Quotes¶

"Happiness does not really depend on objective conditions of either wealth, health or even community. Rather, it depends on the correlation between objective conditions and subjective expectations."

"A meaningful life can be extremely satisfying even in the midst of hardship, whereas a meaningless life is a terrible ordeal no matter how comfortable it is."

"Money brings happiness, but only up to a certain point, and beyond that point it has little significance."

Chapter 20: The End of Homo Sapiens¶

The Big Idea: For 4 billion years, all life evolved strictly by natural selection. Now, Homo sapiens is breaking out of Darwin's rules and replacing natural selection with intelligent design, potentially ending our own species and creating something post-human.

Core Concepts & Notes¶

  • Three Ways Homo Sapiens Could Be Replaced:
    1. Biological Engineering: Rewriting DNA, editing embryos, growing replacement organs, or resurrecting extinct species (like Neanderthals or woolly mammoths).
    2. Cyborg Engineering: Merging organic bodies with inorganic parts (bionic limbs controlled by thought, brain-computer interfaces, cochlear implants, direct links to the internet).
    3. Inorganic Life (AI & Digital Consciousness): Completely non-organic entities (computer programs, neural networks, or scanned human minds) evolving independently of carbon-based biology.
  • The Singularity & Our Blindness: Just as a Neanderthal could never have imagined the stock market or the internet, we cannot comprehend what post-human beings with upgraded brains and emotions will want, think, or value.
  • The Ultimate Question (Afterword: The Animal That Became a God): We have acquired the divine powers of creation and destruction, yet we remain irresponsible, dissatisfied, and confused about our own goals. The real question facing humanity is not "What do we want to become?" but "What do we want to want?"

Mind-Blowing Anecdotes & Stories¶

  • Alba the Fluorescent Green Rabbit (2000): Bio-artist Eduardo Kac commissioned a French lab to splice a jellyfish gene into a white rabbit embryo, creating Alba, a glowing fluorescent green bunny. A playful art stunt that proved humans can now treat the tree of life like a Lego set.
  • Jesse Sullivan & Claudia Mitchell (Bionic Arms): Amputees fitted with robotic arms wired directly into their nervous system; simply by thinking "move my arm", neural signals trigger motors in the prosthetic limb. Meanwhile, US military labs have already tested rats with brain electrodes that can be remote-controlled via joystick.
  • The Frankenstein Prophecy: Mary Shelley's 1818 novel wasn't just a horror story; it was the defining myth of the 21st century, warning that if we try to play God and manufacture a superior being, we may not be able to control what we unleash.

Memorable Quotes¶

"We have mastered our surroundings, increased food production, built cities, established empires and created far-flung trade networks. But did we decrease the amount of suffering in the world?"

"Is there anything more dangerous than dissatisfied and irresponsible gods who don't know what they want?"

"Since we might soon be able to engineer our desires too, perhaps the real question facing us is not 'What do we want to become?', but 'What do we want to want?'"
